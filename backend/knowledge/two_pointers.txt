Two Pointers

The Two Pointers technique uses two indices to process an array or sequence efficiently.

The pointers can move toward each other or move in the same direction depending on the problem.

Common patterns:

1. Opposite-direction pointers:
One pointer starts at the beginning and another at the end.
Move the pointers based on a condition.

2. Same-direction pointers:
Both pointers move from left to right, often at different speeds or representing different positions.

Two Pointers is commonly useful for:
- Searching pairs in sorted arrays.
- Removing duplicates from sorted arrays.
- Reversing arrays or strings.
- Problems involving pairs or ranges.

Time Complexity:
Many Two Pointers solutions run in O(n) because each pointer moves through the array a limited number of times.

Space Complexity:
Usually O(1) when no additional data structure is required.

Common mistakes:
- Moving the wrong pointer.
- Forgetting to move a pointer after processing an element.
- Using Two Pointers when the required input properties are not satisfied.
- Incorrectly handling duplicate values.
